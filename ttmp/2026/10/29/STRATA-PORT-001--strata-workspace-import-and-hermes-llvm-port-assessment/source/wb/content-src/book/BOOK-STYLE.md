# BOOK-STYLE: how *Compilers from First Principles* is written

The book is the workbench's textbook: a preface, 21 chapters in nine parts and two appendices, planned in `book.json`. Each chapter is one Markdown file here, rendered by the Book view with the same renderer as the notes. This page adapts the workbench guide *Writing a book section* (`/home/claude/ds/workbench/form-skate-workbench/writing-a-book-section.md`, read it once) to this book. Where they differ, this page wins.

## Before you write

1. Read your chapter's entry in `book.json`: its fixed `title`, its `about` (the brief: cover all of it), its `presets` and `docs`, and its neighbours' entries.
2. Read `FACTS.md` (generated by `node tools/facts.js`): the running example measured.
3. Read the chapter's presets (`content-src/presets/<id>/`) if they exist yet, `content-src/reference.md`, and the engine code you will explain (`src/core/*.js`, which is concatenated into `site/js/core.js`, and `src/frontend.js`).
4. Write. Touch only your chapter's files.
5. Run `node tools/book-check.js <n>` until it reports no errors, and fix the warnings unless you have a reason.
6. Reread it once as the reader: every term defined before use, every figure matching its caption, every number traceable.

## The reader and the voice

The reader writes JavaScript and has met recursion, hash maps and trees. They have not studied compilers. Sets, functions and orders are introduced where needed; partial orders, lattices and fixed points in Chapter 8; dominance in Chapter 3; refinement in Chapter 10. A chapter that needs an earlier idea links back (`[Chapter 8](lab:book?ch=8)`) instead of teaching it again.

The model is Peter Norvig's technical writing: foundations first, why before how, concrete before abstract, the reader treated as capable.

- **Start with the point.** The first paragraph says what the chapter explains and what the reader will understand by the end. No preamble.
- **Paragraphs develop ideas.** Bullets only for lists of parallel, complete sentences.
- **Why, then how.** Say what problem a mechanism solves and what breaks without it before showing it. When the design chose, name the alternative and why it lost (Hermes does X; the workbench does Y because…).
- **Concrete.** Real IR from the engine, real numbers from a run; every abstract claim gets an example within a paragraph or two.
- **No analogies or metaphors.** Explain a compiler in its own terms.
- **Define terms once**, in bold where they are defined (**safepoint**), then use them the same way everywhere. Use the engine's names: operation, block, terminator, edge, block parameter, cell, behaviour, obligation, permission, contract, variant, fact, lattice, analysis, rewrite, decision, pass, pipeline, strategy, plan, root slot, safepoint, provider, helper, profile, observation, refinement, law, design, objective.
- **British spelling** (behaviour, optimise, analyse, initialise, colour), units with a space ("56 B", "8 KiB", "1,024 slots"). En dashes in ranges.
- **No em dashes.** Use a colon, a comma, parentheses or a new sentence.
- The checker flags: delve, it's worth noting, of course, clearly, obviously, it goes without saying, in this chapter we will, landscape, tapestry, crucial, seamless, robust, leverage, as you can see, it's important to note, imagine, think of it, like a, just like, magic, nuanced, paramount, embark, journey, simply, basically, essentially, in essence, here's the thing, let's dive, unlock, and American spellings of words above.

## What the workbench is, said accurately

The workbench implements the framework in JavaScript: a JavaScript subset frontend, core IR, the reference interpreter, the analyses, rewrites, lowering with root plans, a simulated moving collector, LLVM IR **text** in the shape of the Hermes backend (checked structurally, never compiled), and a real WebAssembly backend for the numeric core. Hermes and LLVM are not in the page; Chapter 16 and Chapter 21 say what changes when the same plans meet them. Never claim the workbench runs LLVM or Hermes code. When you describe Hermes's own backend, describe only what the workbench mirrors (the `shllvm_*` helper shape, the setjmp protocol, the 1,024-slot limit) and say it is the shape the workbench prints.

## Anatomy of a chapter

```text
# <the chapter title, exactly as in book.json>

Opening: one or two paragraphs. What this chapter explains and why a compiler needs it.

## N.1 <first idea>          numbered sections, 4 to 8 of them
## N.2 …
## N.k The running example: <what this chapter shows of the ledger>
## N.m Key points            complete sentences that summarise; nothing new
## Exercises                 3 to 5, from a two-minute edit in a preset to a short project

An optional closing sentence or two connects to the next chapter.
```

- 1,500 to 2,500 words of prose (code, maths and figures do not count; the checker warns under 1,300 and over 3,200). The preface: 900 to 1,500 words, no numbered sections needed. Appendices are as long as their content (Appendix A can be generated from `g.contract.table` and `g.opsets`, verified against the engine).
- At least two figures, numbered N.1, N.2… in order.
- At least one quotation of real engine code, explained line by line after it.
- A link to each of the chapter's presets (`[T-02 Dead code that is not dead](lab:preset?id=T-02)`) and at least one **Try it** callout that loads a preset first.
- Maths derived, not just stated, with a worked example in the engine's numbers.
- Tables for comparisons and for numbers from runs.

## The running example

The ledger (preset B-01, `program.js`, printed with line numbers in FACTS.md) runs through the book. Its output is `rejected: amount must be a number`, `main 42 42`, `audit 42`; it compiles to six functions (`main`, `makeLedger`, `deposit`, `get_balance`, `total`, `arrow`); `deposit` and `total` are the two functions most chapters look at. Each chapter that can has a section "N.k The running example: …" showing its part with real numbers: Chapter 4 the 11 cells promoted and the three that stay, Chapter 9 the live-across sets of `total`, Chapter 13 the 15 root slots and the 68 collections under stress, and so on. For Wasm (Chapter 15) the numeric companion is `fib` in FACTS.md, because the ledger is outside the core profile; say why.

## Figures

A figure is a fenced `svg` block followed by a caption paragraph that starts `**Figure N.k.**`. Technical drawing: hairlines, ink, grey and one accent.

- The block starts with `<svg` and has a `viewBox` at most 720 wide (`viewBox="0 0 640 260"` is typical), with `xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg"`.
- No literal colours: `stroke="currentColor"` or `fill="none"`, and the classes `ink`, `mut`, `acc`, `hair` for strokes; `f-ink`, `f-mut`, `f-acc`, `f-fill`, `f-fill2`, `f-paper`, `f-sel` for fills; `lbl` (small uppercase label), `it` (variables), `b` (bold) for text; `dash`, `dot` for dashed and dotted lines.
- No `<style>`, `<script>`, `<image>`, `<foreignObject>`; well-formed XML (`&lt;`, `&amp;` in text).
- Text 9 to 13 (`font-size="11"`). Maths in labels as Unicode (⊑, ⊔, ⊥, ⊤, ∪, ∖, →, φ, ₀, ₁).
- Weights: 1 for outlines, 1.6 for the subject, 0.6 for construction.
- Arrowheads: a `<marker id="arrow">` with an `f-ink` path; ids may repeat across figures.
- A figure that shows data plots real numbers (FACTS.md or your own run). A schematic says so in its caption if it could be mistaken for data.
- Good subjects: a CFG with block parameters on its edges; a dominator tree; a lattice's Hasse diagram; the worklist's iterations as a table-like grid; a frame layout (root slots, scratch, discard, argv); a timeline of collections and a stale use; the Wasm dispatch loop's nesting; a product of two state machines.

## Code

A `js` block whose first line is `// core.js` or `// frontend.js` is a **quotation**: every other line must appear in that file (whitespace ignored; `site/js/core.js` and `site/js/frontend.js`). Leave lines out with a line that is just `// …`. A preset's file is quoted as `// B-01/program.js`. Keep quotations to 5 to 30 lines and explain them in prose afterwards.

Code that is not a quotation (a sketch, an exercise's start) has no file comment, must run against the real API (check names in `content-src/reference.md` and the engine), and is labelled as yours in the prose. Core IR goes in `text` blocks; LLVM IR in `llvm` blocks; WAT in `wasm` blocks; pseudocode in `text` blocks. IR you show must be what the engine prints: paste it from a run (`program.ir('L1', { fn })`), do not hand-write it.

## Links into the workbench

`lab:` links make the book a workbench manual. The checker resolves every link: preset ids, chapters (and `&at=` headings), docs, files of the chapter's presets, function names and op ids in those presets' programs, levels, overlays, views. A Try it loads the preset first, then acts:

```markdown
> **Try it.** [Load T-02](lab:preset?id=T-02), [select the getter's js.get](lab:node?id=main/try3/0)
> and [run it](lab:build): the line `getter ran` is gone.
```

Op ids (`fn/block/index`) must match the preset's program exactly; take them from `program.cfg(fn)` or the Stage. Write link text that still reads as prose.

## Maths

KaTeX. Inline `$\mathrm{live}_{in}(b) = \mathrm{use}(b) \cup (\mathrm{live}_{out}(b) \setminus \mathrm{def}(b))$` with no space just inside the dollars and no letter right after the closing one; display maths between lines of `$$`. Inside a table write `|` as `\vert`. A dollar sign that is not maths is `\$`.

## Facts

Every number about the engine comes from the engine: FACTS.md, or a measurement you describe ("measured with `node tools/harness.js`", "the Plan view of L-03"). Real-world statements (Hermes's backend limits a frame to 1,024 slots; LLVM's mem2reg uses phi nodes; V8 and SpiderMonkey use moving collectors) can come from general knowledge, stated carefully. Cite only classics you are sure of (Kildall 1973; Cytron, Ferrante, Rosen, Wegman and Zadeck 1991; Cousot and Cousot 1977; Appel's *Modern Compiler Implementation*; Lattner and Adve 2004 for LLVM; Claessen and Hughes 2000 for QuickCheck; Zeller's delta debugging). If the code disagrees with this page, a doc or a note, the code wins: write what the code does and report the difference.

## What the checker enforces

`node tools/book-check.js <n>`: **errors** for line 1 not `# Title`, an unclosed fence, maths KaTeX cannot parse, an SVG that is not well-formed or uses colours or forbidden elements, a `lab:` link that does not resolve, a quotation line not in its file, the renderer throwing. **Warnings** for a title different from the plan's, sections not numbered N.1… in order (fewer than three), no Key points, no running example section, no Exercises, under 1,300 or over 3,200 words, fewer than two figures, a figure without its caption number, a chapter preset never linked, no Try it, no quotation in Parts I to IX, flagged phrases, em dashes, American spellings, a stray `$`.
